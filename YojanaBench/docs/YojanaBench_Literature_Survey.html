LITERATURE SURVEY

Sr. No. Paper Name Authors Year Gap in Existing Solution Proposed Solution in YOJANABENCH
1 GAIA: A Benchmark for General AI Assistants Grégoire Mialon, Clémentine Fourrier, Craig Swift, Thomas Wolf, Yann LeCun, Thomas Scialom 2023 General-purpose English assistant benchmark with 466 real-world questions; tests reasoning, multimodality and tool use, but has no domain documents, no eligibility or entitlement reasoning, no verdict ground truth, and is not India-specific. YojanaBench is a domain-specific benchmark built on real Indian welfare-scheme guideline PDFs with human-verified QA pairs, eligible/ineligible verdict references, and numerical entitlement targets — a task class GAIA does not cover.
2 LawBench: Benchmarking Legal Knowledge of Large Language Models Zhiwei Fei, Xiaoyu Shen, Dawei Zhu, Fengzhe Zhou, Zhuo Han, Alan Huang, Songyang Zhang, Kai Chen, Zhixin Yin, Zongwen Shen, Jidong Ge, Vincent Ng 2024 Covers 20 Chinese legal tasks across Bloom’s cognitive levels, but is limited to Chinese law; contains no welfare-scheme documents, no benefit-amount computation, no counterfactual eligibility scenarios, and no multi-agent evaluation pipeline. YojanaBench targets Indian welfare-scheme document reasoning: eligibility clauses, entitlement formulas, document checklists and appeal steps with counterfactual edge cases, evaluated through a multi-agent audit pipeline rather than single-model prompting.
3 DocVQA: A Dataset for VQA on Document Images Minesh Mathew, Dimosthenis Karatzas, C.V. Jawahar 2021 Provides 50,000 extractive QA pairs over 12,000+ document images, but answers are span extractions only — no eligibility verdicts, no numerical entitlement computation, no counterfactual scenarios, and no clause-level ground-truth evidence. YojanaBench moves beyond extraction to reasoning: every QA item carries a verdict (eligible / ineligible / needs_info), an entitlement value, cited evidence clause IDs, and perturbed counterfactual variants that test boundary conditions.
4 LegalBench: A Collaboratively Built Benchmark for Measuring Legal Reasoning in Large Language Models Neel Guha, Julian Nyarko, Daniel E. Ho, Christopher Ré, Adam Chilton, Alex Chohlas-Wood, Austin Peters, Brandon Waldon, Daniel Rockmore, Diego Zambrano, et al. 2024 Measures US legal reasoning through 162 tasks, but is confined to American case law and contracts; it does not address government benefit rules, document-compliance checklists, or numeric entitlement calculation. YojanaBench supplies what LegalBench lacks for the governance domain: scheme-rule reasoning with required-document verification, benefit computation accuracy, and an appeal-routing task tied to Indian administrative hierarchies.
5 Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Large Language Models: A Survey Yunfan Gao, Yun Xiong, Xinyu Gao, Kangxiang Jia, Jinliu Pan, Yuxi Bi, Yi Dai, Jiawei Sun, Meng Wang, Haofen Wang 2023 Surveys Naive, Advanced and Modular RAG paradigms and their evaluation, but offers no domain benchmark for governance documents; evaluation lacks verdict-level and computation-level metrics needed for high-stakes compliance decisions. YojanaBench applies section-aware, schema-structured RAG over scheme PDFs and defines compliance-specific metrics (verdict accuracy, entitlement error, document F1, confidence calibration) that go beyond standard RAG answer-similarity scores.
6 Large Language Model based Multi-Agents: A Survey of Progress and Challenges Taicheng Guo, Xiuying Chen, Yaqi Wang, Ruidi Chang, Shichao Pei, Nitesh V. Chawla, Olaf Wiest, Xiangliang Zhang 2024 Systematically surveys LLM multi-agent profiling, communication and capability acquisition, but lists no welfare-governance application and no verified benchmark on which an audit-style agent pipeline can be scored against ground truth. YojanaBench instantiates a concrete 8-agent audit pipeline (Corpus, Intake, RAG, Entitlement, Verification, Orchestrator, Evaluation, Appeal) with a human-approval gate, and releases the benchmark needed to reproducibly score multi-agent compliance systems.
7 Need of Retrieval Augmented Generation for Conversational Assistants for Government Social Welfare Scheme Popularization Vaidheeswaran Vaishnav, Guru Omkar Nathan 2024 Builds a RAG chatbot over 2,980 scraped government schemes and measures similarity, uncertainty and hallucination — but has no human-verified QA ground truth, no eligibility verdict labels, no entitlement computation checks, and no counterfactual/edge-case evaluation. YojanaBench provides the missing evaluation standard: human-verified QA pairs and verdict references over 10–15 curated scheme guideline PDFs, with EM / F1 / verdict-accuracy / entitlement-error scoring and ablation of each agent.
8 First Impressions from Comparing Form-Based and Conversational Interfaces for Public Service Access in India Chaitra C R, Pranathi Voora, Bhaskar Ruthvik Bikkina, Bharghavaram Boddapati, Srivant Vishnuvajjala, Vivan Jain, Prajna Upadhyay, Dipanjan Chakraborty 2025 Compares the myScheme form app with a RAG conversational prototype (Prabodhini) on usability with 15 users — evaluates interface preference and task time, but not whether the scheme information returned is correct against official guideline documents. YojanaBench evaluates correctness rather than usability: every response is scored against document-derived ground truth (eligibility, entitlement, documents, appeal), so a fast but wrong answer scores zero — the audit standard missing in interface-only studies.
